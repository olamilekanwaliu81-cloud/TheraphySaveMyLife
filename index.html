Paula's story

A poster on the wall changed her life

"I almost walked past it. But I didn't. I walked into that clinic and met a therapist who saved my life."

In her mid-twenties, Paula had just graduated from NYU. But behind the degree, her world was falling apart. She had a miscarriage. Her boyfriend left her. She grew up with an abusive father and a mother who was deeply depressed. The pain became too heavy to carry, and she started thinking about ending her life.

Then she saw a small poster in her dorm elevator: "NYU Psychological Clinic. If you're having suicidal thoughts, we can help." She almost walked past it. She was scared to walk through that door. But she didn't walk past — and that one brave step changed the rest of her life.

Therapy was slow. It was painful. For years, Paula did the hard work of looking at her childhood and learning why she felt the way she did. Little by little, the darkness lifted. She found her voice through therapy, art, and recovery. She learned to love herself.

She wrote this book so you would know one thing: you are not alone, and healing is possible.

— Paula

About the author

Meet Paula Axelrod

Paula is a survivor, an artist, and a writer. She grew up carrying pain from a difficult childhood, and in her twenties she reached a point where she wanted to end her life. One brave step into a therapist's office changed her story forever.

For many years, she did the deep, patient work of therapy. She learned why she felt the way she did, found her voice through art, and slowly grew into a life she loves. Today she wants to hand other people the hope she once found.

She wrote this memoir — and the free therapist-finding guide — so that no one has to feel as lost and alone as she once did.

With love and hope, Paula
